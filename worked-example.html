The flow

Fourteen steps in four phases

Each step below says what happens, why it is needed, and the check that must pass before the next step. The FEM solver runs in step 7; everything before prepares it and everything after makes its answer trustworthy.

Understand 1 · Prompt → 2 · CAD → 3 · Hand estimate → 4 · Faces → Solve 5 · Mesh → 6 · Equations → 7 · FEM solver → 8 · Answer → Verify 9 · Convergence → 10 · Sanity → Deliver 11 · Files → 12 · Sandbox → 13 · Score → 14 · Review
Understandread the task and the part before computing anything
1

Read the prompt

What happensWrite down every fact the prompt gives: plate 176.914 × 148.475 × 10 mm with a hole; steel, E = 200 000 MPa, ν = 0.3; the face at y = −74.2373 mm fixed; 1000 N spread evenly over the face at y = +74.2373 mm, once along −y (compression) and once along +x (lateral); report the largest movement for each, in mm.

Why it is neededEverything the program does must come from the prompt. Nothing is recalled or guessed, and the units (mm, N, MPa) are fixed here once.

Check, then nextThe numbers go into the top of main.py as named constants. Next: open the CAD file.

2

Open the CAD file

What happenspart.step is a STEP file: exact surfaces, not a mesh. Gmsh opens it through OpenCASCADE and sees 10 points, 15 curves, 7 surfaces (6 flat faces and 1 cylinder, the 20.73 mm hole) and 1 volume.

Why it is neededBefore solving, confirm the file is the part the prompt describes. Astra's bracket failure (case 2) came from a wrong size check at exactly this step.

Check, then nextExact box (Geometry.OCCBoundsUseStl = 1): 176.914 × 148.475 × 10.000 mm, within 0.01 mm of the prompt; one solid; volume 259 298 mm³. Next: estimate the answer.

3

Estimate the answer by hand

What happensCompression: a bar shortens by δ = F·L / (E·A) = 1000 × 148.475 / (200 000 × 1769.14) = 4.20 × 10⁻⁴ mm. Lateral: a short, deep cantilever bends (F·L³ / 3EI = 1.18 × 10⁻³ mm) and shears (F·L / κGA = 1.31 × 10⁻³ mm): about 2.49 × 10⁻³ mm.

Why it is neededKnow the expected size before trusting any solver. Astra's buckling failure (case 1) is exactly a solver number nobody compared with a hand estimate.

Check, then nextThe FEM answer should be of this size and a little larger, since the hole and the clamped end make the plate softer. Next: find the faces.

4

Find the support and load faces

What happensAmong the flat faces with area 176.914 × 10 = 1769.14 mm², take the one whose centre is at y = −74.2373 (surface 5, fixed) and the one at y = +74.2373 (surface 3, loaded).

Why it is neededThe prompt names faces by position, not by a CAD number. Face numbers change between programs; positions do not.

Check, then nextBoth faces found, area 1769.14 mm² each. Next: mesh.

Solveturn the part into equations and solve them
5

Mesh the part

What happensGmsh cuts the volume into tetrahedra at three sizes: h = 10, 7 and 5 mm (2 361, 4 662 and 12 482 elements). scikit-fem then uses quadratic (P2) displacement on straight edges: 10 nodes per element.

Why it is neededFEM solves the physics on small pieces. Quadratic elements follow bending well. Straight edges cannot fold inside out, the bug that broke Astra's finer bracket mesh.

Check, then nextEvery element has positive volume. Mesh volume 259 386 mm³, 0.03 % above the CAD (the hole is made of flat facets). Next: build the equations.

6

Build the equations

What happensStiffness matrix K from linear elasticity (Lamé constants from E and ν), integrated exactly for quadratic elements. Load vector f from a uniform pull of F / A = 0.565 MPa on the loaded face. All movement on the fixed face is set to zero.

Why it is neededK·u = f is equilibrium written for every node: internal spring forces balance the applied load. Supports and loads turn the general K into this task.

Check, then nextf adds up to exactly 1000 N in the load direction. 68 046 unknowns on the finest mesh. Next: solve.

K = asm(linear_elasticity(lam, mu), basis)        # stiffness
f = asm(traction, loaded_face)                    # 1000 N spread over the face
u[free] = splu(K[free][:, free]).solve(f[free])  # fixed face held at zero
7

Solve with the FEM solver

What happensSciPy's sparse LU (SuperLU) factorises the reduced K once, then solves for both load cases.

Why it is neededThis is the FEM solve itself. A direct solver is exact up to rounding, and one factorisation serves both loads.

Check, then nextResidual ‖K·u − f‖ / ‖f‖ ≈ 10⁻¹². Reaction at the fixed face = −1000 N in each case: force balance holds to 10⁻¹³. Next: read the answer.

8

Read off the answer

What happensCompute the movement |u| at every node and take the largest for each load case.

Why it is neededThe prompt asks for the maximum displacement magnitude, nothing else.

Check, then nextCompression 4.3848 × 10⁻⁴ mm, lateral 3.0283 × 10⁻³ mm, both at the loaded end (pictures below). Next: check the mesh does not matter.

Verifyconvince yourself the number is right
9

Check convergence

What happensCompare the three meshes. Compression: 4.3575 → 4.3767 → 4.3848 × 10⁻⁴ mm (changes 0.44 %, then 0.19 %). Lateral: 3.0122 → 3.0229 → 3.0283 × 10⁻³ mm (0.35 %, then 0.18 %).

Why it is neededAn answer that moves when the mesh is refined is not an answer. The benchmark asks for less than 1 % change between the two finest meshes.

Check, then next0.19 % and 0.18 %: converged. Report the finest mesh. Next: compare with the hand estimate.

10

Compare with the hand estimate

What happensCompression comes out 4.5 % above F·L/(E·A); lateral 22 % above the beam estimate.

Why it is neededA big mismatch would mean a unit, support or load error. A small one should have a physical reason.

Check, then nextBoth are larger, as expected: the hole removes material and the clamped end restrains the plate in a way beam formulas ignore. Next: write the files.

Deliverhand over the work and let it be checked
11

Write the eight files

What happensresults.json (the two values), main.py, part.step (the supplied CAD, unchanged), mesh.msh (finest mesh), fields.npz (displacements), solver.log, refinement.json (three meshes), environment.json (software versions).

Why it is neededThe numbers alone are not proof. These files let anyone rerun, inspect and audit the work.

Check, then next{"outputs": {"max_displacement_compression_mm": 0.000438478, "max_displacement_lateral_mm": 0.003028335}}. Next: replay.

12

Replay offline in the sandbox

What happensThe benchmark's own sandbox_runner.py runs main.py once: no network, read-only inputs, 600 s, 8 GiB, 2 threads.

Why it is neededProves the program runs by itself with only the given inputs: no hidden files, no internet, no hand edits.

Check, then nextFinished in 11.1 s, exit code 0, all eight files written. Next: score.

13

Score with the benchmark scorer

What happensThe benchmark's own score.py compares each value with the hidden reference and checks the files.

Why it is neededThe same scorer is used for every model, so this run is directly comparable.

Check, then nextRelative errors 0.25 % and 0.33 % against a ±5 % band: numbers pass, score 1.0. All 8 files present; part.step is 1 valid solid. Next: review.

14

Expert review (still to do)

What happensExpert human annotators check five things against the file hashes: the run (log, exit code), the geometry (the right faces fixed and loaded), the setup (E, ν, 1000 N, supports), the fields (smooth, largest at the loaded end) and convergence (three meshes, under 1 %).

Why it is neededCorrect numbers can come from a wrong setup by luck. The review confirms the work behind them.

Check, then nextNot done yet, so the scorer reports trusted workflow review missing: numbers pass, end-to-end pass waits for the review.

Limits

What this example does and does not show

A simple task

We chose an easy task on purpose, to show the method. It says nothing about how hard the benchmark is.

Solved by a person with tools

We wrote and ran the code with full access to tools and error messages. A model in the benchmark gets one try and no feedback.

The reference was not used

The program never reads the reference. Mesh sizes were fixed before the first run and not changed afterwards; we compared with the reference only at the end.

Not yet a full pass

The numbers pass, but the expert human annotator review has not been done, so the scorer does not count it as passed end to end.

Exact prompt
Holed steel plate. part.step is one solid in millimetres: a rectangular plate 176.914 mm (x) by 148.475 mm (y) by 10.000 mm (z) with a through hole along z.
Material: ANSYS default Structural Steel, linear isotropic elastic, E = 200 GPa, Poisson ratio 0.3. Linear static, small displacements, no gravity.
Supports: the end face at y = -74.2373 mm is fully fixed (all displacement components zero).
Two separate load cases, each a total force of 1000 N applied as a uniform traction over the opposite end face (y = 74.2373 mm):
  (1) compression: 1000 N along -y (towards the fixed end);
  (2) lateral bending: 1000 N along +x (in-plane shear of the free end).

Outputs:
- max_displacement_compression_mm [mm]: maximum displacement magnitude, load case 1
- max_displacement_lateral_mm [mm]: maximum displacement magnitude, load case 2

Submit results.json containing exactly an outputs object with these numeric keys. Also submit main.py, part.step, mesh.msh, fields.npz, solver.log, refinement.json and environment.json.
